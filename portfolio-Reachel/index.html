01 Nature × Mathematics

Nature& Order

Does beauty begin with order?

Flower study01-a
Leaf, hatched01-b
Geometry01-c
Wave form01-d
Observation
I began by watching plants — the branching of a tree, the arrangement of petals, the spiral hidden in a flower head. Order kept appearing without being asked to.
Process
Field drawings were made on site; then the same forms were redrawn as pure structure — nodes, edges, proportions — to test whether the eye was reading a real pattern or inventing one.
Reflection
I started noticing order in nature. The question this planted: if order is everywhere, does the mind enjoy beauty because it recognises the rules — or because it makes them? Fibonacci spiral → ratio 1 : 1.618
02 Mathematics × The Human Face

MeasuringBeauty

Can mathematics describe beauty?

Left eye02-a
Right eye02-b
Lips02-c
Hypothesis
Beauty in a face may follow golden-ratio proportions and bilateral symmetry.
Measurement
Grids were laid over the face; ratios between features and symmetry across the midline were measured by hand.
Observation
Eyes are not perfectly 1 : 1.618. Real faces are not perfectly symmetrical. But they can still be beautiful.
Open thread
If the measurements fail and the beauty remains — then where does the beauty live? Why ? → carried into study 03
03 Psychology × Self

Perception& Self

What changes when visual judgment is removed?

The face I feel03-a
Figure study03-b
Eye: hands inside03-c
Hand & treble clef03-d
Protocol
Close your eyes → touch your own face → draw what you perceive → place two definitions side by side.
The comparison
Society’s Beauty — thinness, standard features, symmetry rules, flawlessness.  vs.  My Beauty — piercings, my own asymmetry, details I personally find beautiful.
Society’s Beauty My Beauty
Reflection
Without sight, the face became a collection of pressures, edges and memories. The two drawings are not two portraits — they are two definitions of beauty facing each other.
